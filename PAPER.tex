\begin{abstract}
Can hidden Markov architectures of increasing size reproduce a fixed observed process with arbitrarily small dissipation? For finite-realizable stationary two-label alternating-renewal laws, we prove that zero all-dimension cost is equivalent to both complete dwell laws being finite positive mixtures of exponential densities, and to the existence of a finite reversible exact realization. The necessity extends the established equilibrium spectral restriction to vanishing-dissipation sequences of unbounded dimension and unrestricted rates. Comparing a chain with its additive reversibilization converts negative correlation between bounded past and future persistence tests into a dimension-independent dissipation certificate. For a specified three-state-ring observation at $q=1/20$, an exact-arithmetic fixed-window certificate gives $V_\infty>0.017568198744$ in target time units, with Boltzmann's constant one. We also give an example beyond the elementary variance obstruction and a certificate stable under bounded observational errors. The exact positive optimum, its finite-dimensional attainment, and statistical confidence-set construction remain unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question and its resolution}
Let $Q$ be a finite irreducible continuous-time Markov generator in row convention, $\pi Q=0$, and let $h$ assign a visible label to every microscopic state. The chain starts in its stationary law. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epr}
 F_{ij}=\pi_iQ_{ij},\qquad
 \sig(Q)=\sum_{i<j}(F_{ij}-F_{ji})\log\frac{F_{ij}}{F_{ji}}.
\end{equation}
A missing reverse edge carrying positive forward flux gives infinite cost. Zero flux in both directions gives zero contribution. All logarithms are natural. Ordinary time reversal is used; microscopic variables are even under time reversal.

For a stationary observed path law $P$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:V}
 V_n(P)=\inf\{\sig(Q): |Q|\leq n,\ P_{Q,h}=P\},
 \qquad V_\infty(P)=\inf_nV_n(P).
\end{equation}
Equality $P_{Q,h}=P$ means equality of the entire stationary observed path law, not merely a collection of moments. The target and these conventions are those of Aznagulov \cite{aznagulov}. Its unpriced zero-limit question is resolved here without using its fixed-dimension compactification claims.

\begin{theorem}[Main result]\label{thm:main}
For the alternating-renewal law $P_q$ defined in Section~\ref{sec:target}, and every $0<q<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:allq}
0<\frac{2(1-q)^4}{3(3q+5)^2(2q^2+3q+3)^2}
\leq V_\infty(P_q)\leq(1-q)\log(1/q).
\end{equation}
At $q=1/20$ the following stronger rational certificate holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mainconstant}
V_\infty(P_{1/20})\geq
\frac{182774933998705388800}{22434692075028399713067}
>\frac1{125}.
\end{equation}
Every inequality is valid over the unrestricted finite deterministic-observation class, including realizations with multiple microscopic states under either visible label.
\end{theorem}

The lower bounds are not asserted to be sharp. In particular, this theorem decides whether the all-dimension infimum is zero, but does not distinguish a positive minimum attained at finite dimension from a positive infimum approached only at growing dimension.

The central structural result is Theorem~\ref{thm:dichotomy}: for every finite-realizable stationary ergodic two-label alternating-renewal law,
\[
\begin{aligned}
V_\infty(P)=0
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{both dwell laws are hyperexponential}\\
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{a finite reversible exact realization exists}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here hyperexponential means a finite positive mixture of exponential densities, not an arbitrary phase-type law. The equilibrium mixture restriction is classical; the extra implication is that a sequence of exact realizations with vanishing dissipation must enter this class even without a common bound on dimension or rates. Section~\ref{sec:beyondvariance} illustrates a case not detected by the elementary dwell-variance test.

\section{The target law and an exact finite competitor}\label{sec:target}
For $q>0$ put
\[
 s=1+q,\qquad c=1+q+q^2,\qquad
 D_q(z)=z^2+2sz+c.
\]
The complete $A$ visits are exponential with rate $s$. The complete $B$ visits have Laplace transform
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f}
 \widehat f_q(z)=\frac{(2q/s)z+c}{D_q(z)}.
\end{equation}
Successive visits are independent and alternate between $A$ and $B$. The stationary version of this renewal process defines $P_q$.

These specifications have the exact three-state realization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ring}
 Q_q=\begin{pmatrix}-s&1&q\\q&-s&1\\1&q&-s\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \pi=(1/3,1/3,1/3),
 \qquad h(0)=A,\quad h(1)=h(2)=B.
\end{equation}
For a complete $B$ visit the entrance distribution, killed generator, and exit rates are
\[
 \alpha=(1/s,q/s),\qquad
 T=\begin{pmatrix}-s&1\\q&-s\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 t=(q,1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Direct inversion gives $\alpha(zI-T)^{-1}t=\widehat f_q(z)$. The single $A$ state resets the process on every return; the holding time in $A$ is independent of its exit destination. Thus the construction realizes the complete alternating-renewal law, not just its marginal waiting-time distributions.

Differentiating \eqref{eq:f} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:means}
 \E T_A=1/s,\qquad \E T_B=2/s,\qquad \E T_B^2=6/c.
\end{equation}
The stationary $B$ probability is $p=2/3$ and the rate of entrances into each block is $r=s/3$. Each of the three unoriented edges in \eqref{eq:ring} carries opposing fluxes $1/3$ and $q/3$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper}
 \sig(Q_q)=(1-q)\log(1/q),
\end{equation}
proving the upper bound in \eqref{eq:allq}. In particular, the all-dimension infimum is finite. At $q=1$ the $B$ dwell reduces to an exponential of rate one; a reversible two-state chain realizes the target.

\section{Small entropy production forces proximity to hidden equilibrium}
For an arbitrary realization $Q$, define its stationary reverse and additive reversibilization by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R}
 Q^\dagger=D_\pi^{-1}Q^{\mathsf T}D_\pi,
 \qquad R=\tfrac12(Q+Q^\dagger).
\end{equation}
The generator $R$ is irreducible and reversible for $\pi$. It has the same diagonal entries as $Q$ and therefore the same exit rate from every microscopic state. It is a comparison chain, not an exact realization of the target.

\begin{lemma}[Dimension-free path comparison]\label{lem:path}
Let $\mathcal P_Q^T$ and $\mathcal P_R^T$ be the stationary microscopic path laws on a time interval of length $T$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pathkl}
 \KL(\mathcal P_Q^T\Vert\mathcal P_R^T)
 \leq\frac{T}{4}\sig(Q).
\end{equation}
If two independent exponential clocks of rate $z>0$ delimit the interval $[-E_-,E_+]$, independently of the chain, the corresponding joint laws of clocks and path satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:randomkl}
 \KL(\mathcal P_Q^{\mathrm{clock}}\Vert
             \mathcal P_R^{\mathrm{clock}})
 \leq \frac{\sig(Q)}{2z}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The initial stationary laws coincide, and the holding-time terms cancel because the exit rates agree. The standard jump-path likelihood ratio, or its direct product-of-rates calculation, gives
\[
 \frac1T\KL(\mathcal P_Q^T\Vert\mathcal P_R^T)
 =\sum_{i\ne j}F_{ij}\log\frac{2F_{ij}}{F_{ij}+F_{ji}}.
\]
For $x,y>0$, the scalar inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar}
 x\log\frac{2x}{x+y}+y\log\frac{2y}{x+y}
 \leq\frac14(x-y)\log(x/y)
\end{equation}
suffices. To verify it, set $m=(x+y)/2$ and $u=(x-y)/(x+y)$. The right side minus the left, divided by $m$, is
\[
 L(u)=-\log(1-u^2)-u\operatorname{artanh}u.
\]
This function is even and $L(0)=0$. For $0\leq u<1$,
\[
 L'(u)=\frac{u}{1-u^2}-\operatorname{artanh}u\geq0,
\]
since the expression on the right vanishes at zero and has derivative $2u^2/(1-u^2)^2$. This proves \eqref{eq:scalar}. If $\sig(Q)=\infty$, the claimed bound is immediate; otherwise all used edges have reverse support, and summing \eqref{eq:scalar} proves \eqref{eq:pathkl}.

Condition on the independently drawn clock lengths and use stationarity to translate the interval to one beginning at zero. The mean total length is $2/z$. Integrating \eqref{eq:pathkl} proves \eqref{eq:randomkl}. No optional-stopping identity or assumption about independent path increments is being used.
\end{proof}

The factor $1/4$ is a rate-level comparison with the generator $R$. It is not a statement that the path law of $R$ is the arithmetic mixture of the forward and reversed path laws.

\section{Two observable clocks and an equilibrium restriction}\label{sec:clocks}
Let $B$ be any nontrivial observed block, with stationary probability $p$. Define its stationary persistence function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 H(t)=\Prob\{Y_u=B\ \text{for every }0\leq u\leq t\},\qquad H(0)=p.
\end{equation}
Independently draw $E_-,E_+\sim\operatorname{Exp}(z)$. Conditional on $Y_0=B$, define the binary tests
\begin{align*}
 U&=\one\{Y_u=B\text{ throughout }[-E_-,0]\},\\
 V&=\one\{Y_u=B\text{ throughout }[0,E_+]\}.
\end{align*}
The tests concern an uninterrupted visit. Seeing $B$ at the two endpoints alone is not sufficient. Hidden jumps within $B$ are allowed. The clocks are an external mathematical sampling device, not a modification of the physical realization.

Stationarity gives equal marginals. Writing $\widehat H(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{-zt}H(t)\,dt$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ab}
 a=\Prob(U=1\mid B)=\Prob(V=1\mid B)=\frac{z\widehat H(z)}p,
 \qquad b=\Prob(U=V=1\mid B)=-\frac{z^2\widehat H'(z)}p.
\end{equation}
For the joint event, stationarity gives persistence over an interval of length $E_-+E_+$, whose density is $z^2te^{-zt}$. Thus \eqref{eq:ab} follows directly, without a renewal assumption.

\begin{lemma}[Equilibrium has nonnegative clock covariance]\label{lem:cov}
For every finite reversible hidden chain with deterministic observation, the two-clock probabilities satisfy $b\geq a^2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Conditional on a present microscopic state $X_0=i$, the stationary past and future are independent. Reversibility makes their conditional laws identical. After including the independent clocks, both binary tests have the same conditional success probability $v_i\in[0,1]$. Hence, conditional on $Y_0=B$,
\[
 a=\sum_{i\in B}\frac{\pi_i}{p}v_i,
 \qquad b=\sum_{i\in B}\frac{\pi_i}{p}v_i^2,
\]
and the assertion is the nonnegativity of the variance of $v_i$.
\end{proof}

\section{A quantitative information-projection lemma}
Suppose $0<a<1$, $b<a^2$, and set $\delta=a^2-b>0$. The four conditional probabilities, ordered as $00,01,10,11$, are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:w}
 w=\big((1-a)^2-\delta,\ a(1-a)+\delta,\ a(1-a)+\delta,\ a^2-\delta\big).
\end{equation}
Assume they are positive; boundary cases follow by limits when needed. Let
\[
 r_a=\big((1-a)^2,\ a(1-a),\ a(1-a),\ a^2\big),
 \qquad I(a,b)=\KL(w\Vert r_a).
\]
The quantity $I(a,b)$ is the mutual information of the two binary tests.

\begin{lemma}[Nearest positively correlated symmetric binary law]\label{lem:projection}
For any valid symmetric binary law
\[
 r_{u,v}=(1-2u+v,u-v,u-v,v),\qquad v\geq u^2,
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection}
 \KL(w\Vert r_{u,v})\geq I(a,b).
\end{equation}
Thus the closest law in this class, in the stated direction of relative entropy, is the independent law with the target marginal $a$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If a zero coordinate of $r_{u,v}$ has positive $w$ mass, the claim is immediate. Otherwise $-\log x\geq1-x$ gives
\begin{align*}
 \KL(w\Vert r_{u,v})-\KL(w\Vert r_a)
 &\geq1-\sum_j w_j\frac{(r_{u,v})_j}{(r_a)_j}\\
 &=\frac{\delta\big[(v-u^2)+(u-a)^2\big]}{a^2(1-a)^2}\geq0.
\end{align*}
Equality is attained at $u=a,v=a^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[An exact rational lower bound for the information]\label{lem:cubic}
For $\delta=a^2-b>0$ as above,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubicI}
 I(a,b)\geq
 \frac{\delta^2}{2a^2(1-a)^2}
 +\frac{(2a-1)^2\delta^3}{6a^4(1-a)^4}.
\end{equation}
In particular $I(a,b)\geq8\delta^2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Keep $a$ fixed and replace $\delta$ in \eqref{eq:w} by $x\in[0,\delta]$. Let the resulting information be $I(x)$. Then $I(0)=I'(0)=0$, and
\[
 I''(x)=\frac1{(1-a)^2-x}+\frac2{a(1-a)+x}+\frac1{a^2-x}.
\]
Use, on their respective domains,
\[
 \frac1{r-x}\geq\frac1r+\frac{x}{r^2},
 \qquad \frac1{r+x}\geq\frac1r-\frac{x}{r^2}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 I''(x)\geq C_0+C_1x,\quad
 C_0=\frac1{a^2(1-a)^2},\quad
 C_1=\frac{(2a-1)^2}{a^4(1-a)^4}.
\]
Twice integrating proves \eqref{eq:cubicI}. Finally $a(1-a)\leq1/4$ gives $C_0/2\geq8$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dimension-free observable dissipation certificate]\label{thm:certificate}
For any finite stationary hidden Markov realization and the observed two-clock statistics above, if $b<a^2$ then
\begin{align}
 \sig(Q)&\geq2zp\,I(a,b),\label{eq:MIbound}\\
 \sig(Q)&\geq zp\frac{\delta^2}{a^2(1-a)^2}
 +\frac{zp}{3}\frac{(2a-1)^2\delta^3}{a^4(1-a)^4}.
 \label{eq:rationalbound}
\end{align}
The bound requires no ceiling on hidden dimension, rates, or stationary activity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compare $Q$ with its reversibilization $R$ on the random two-sided interval. Both chains have the same stationary mass $p$ in $B$. Apply data processing to the record consisting of the event $Y_0\in B$ and, on that event, the two tests. The chain rule for relative entropy and Lemma~\ref{lem:path} give
\[
 \frac{\sig(Q)}{2z}\geq p\,\KL(w\Vert w_R).
\]
Under $R$, Lemma~\ref{lem:cov} gives the required symmetric positively correlated law $w_R$. Lemma~\ref{lem:projection} proves \eqref{eq:MIbound}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:cubic} proves \eqref{eq:rationalbound}. All statements hold for an arbitrary original realization; $R$ need not reproduce the target law.
\end{proof}

\section{Evaluation for the full family and the specified parameter}
For an alternating-renewal process, a stationary time falls uniformly along a length-biased visit. Equivalently, the expected fraction of starting times for which an interval of length $t$ lies in a complete $B$ visit of duration $u$ is proportional to $(u-t)_+$. Therefore
\[
 H_q(t)=r\int_t^\infty(u-t)f_q(u)\,du,
 \qquad r=s/3.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:f} gives the particularly simple transform
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hq}
 \widehat H_q(z)=\frac{p}{z}-\frac{r(1-\widehat f_q(z))}{z^2}
 =\frac{2z+3s}{3D_q(z)}.
\end{equation}
Substitution in \eqref{eq:ab} yields
\begin{align}
 a_q(z)&=\frac{z(2z+3s)}{2D_q(z)},\label{eq:aq}\\
 b_q(z)&=\frac{z^2(z+q+2)(z+2q+1)}{D_q(z)^2},\label{eq:bq}\\
 \delta_q(z)&=a_q(z)^2-b_q(z)
 =\frac{z^2(1-q)^2}{4D_q(z)^2}>0\quad(q\ne1).
 \label{eq:deltaq}
\end{align}
Thus every positive clock rate supplies an observable equilibrium obstruction.

Keeping only the quadratic term of \eqref{eq:rationalbound} gives, for every $z>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:familybound}
 V_\infty(P_q)\geq
 \frac{2z^3(1-q)^4}{3(2z+3s)^2(sz+2c)^2}.
\end{equation}
Setting $z=1$ proves the lower bound in \eqref{eq:allq}.

For the concrete target, choose the fixed clock rate $z=6$. Exact arithmetic gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:numbers}
 p=\frac23,\qquad
 a=\frac{18180}{19861},\qquad
 b=\frac{329212800}{394459321},\qquad
 \delta=\frac{1299600}{394459321}.
\end{equation}
The four joint probabilities and their independent comparison probabilities share the denominator $394459321=19861^2$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
& $00$ & $01$ & $10$ & $11$\\
\midrule
Target numerator & 1526161 & 31860180 & 31860180 & 329212800\\
Independent numerator & 2825761 & 30560580 & 30560580 & 330512400\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Equation~\eqref{eq:rationalbound} is now a calculation involving rational numbers only. It gives
\[
 \sig(Q)\geq
 \frac{182774933998705388800}{22434692075028399713067}
 =0.008146977608939347\ldots.
\]
The strict margin over $1/125$ is exactly
\[
 \frac{412174674809773886933}{2804336509378549964133375}>0.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:mainconstant} and finishes Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

Retaining the logarithms in \eqref{eq:MIbound}, rather than the rational underestimate, gives the stronger explicit certificate
\begin{align*}
 V_\infty(P_{1/20})\geq \frac{8}{394459321}\bigg[&
 1526161\log\frac{1526161}{2825761}
 +63720360\log\frac{31860180}{30560580}\\
 &+329212800\log\frac{329212800}{330512400}\bigg]
 =0.0084471687960995336\ldots.
\end{align*}
A rational series with an explicit remainder certifies this numerical enclosure in the supplied verifier. This is a certified lower bound, not the evaluated optimum.

\subsection{A stronger fixed-window certificate}\label{sec:fixed}
The exponential clocks are not essential. For any $t>0$, use persistence on $[-t,0]$ and $[0,t]$, conditional on $Y_0=B$, and set $a_t=H(t)/p$, $b_t=H(2t)/p$.
\begin{corollary}[Fixed-window bound]\label{cor:fixed}
If $b_t<a_t^2$, then
\[
\sig(Q)\geq \frac{2p}{t}I(a_t,b_t).
\]
The same finite stationary deterministic-observation assumptions apply; renewal is not required.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The comparison path interval has length $2t$. Lemma~\ref{lem:path} and conditional data processing give $p\KL(w\Vert w_R)\leq t\sig(Q)/2$. Given the current microscopic state, reversible past and future have the same independent persistence law, so Lemmas~\ref{lem:cov} and~\ref{lem:projection} apply without change.
\end{proof}
For the ring target, $g(t)=H_q(t)/p=\tfrac12\one^{\mathsf T}e^{tT}\one$. At $q=1/20$ and $t=3/10$, exact rational enclosures certify
\[
0.017568198744 < B_{3/10}:=\frac{2p}{t}I(g(t),g(2t)) < 0.017568198745.
\]
Thus $V_\infty(P_{1/20})>0.017568198744$, improving the displayed exponential-clock certificate by a factor of about $2.080$. This extension was suggested by the supplied referee audit and incorporated in version 1.1.

For reproducibility, put $x=st$ and $C=I+T/s$, which is substochastic. Uniformisation gives $g(t)=e^{-x}\sum_{k\geq0}(x^k/k!)c_k$, with $c_k=\tfrac12\one^{\mathsf T}C^k\one\in[0,1]$. For both required times, $x\leq1$. Consecutive alternating Taylor sums enclose $e^{-x}$; truncating the positive series at $N=32$ leaves a tail at most
\[
\frac{x^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}\left(1-\frac{x}{N+2}\right)^{-1}.
\]
For rational $r>0$, $y=(r-1)/(r+1)$ gives $\log r=2\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}y^{2k+1}/(2k+1)+R_m$, with
$|R_m|\leq2|y|^{2m+1}/((2m+1)(1-y^2))$.
Rational interval operations certify the enclosure, including negative logarithmic terms (the supplied audit uses $m=32$ and the release implementation uses $m=40$). The upper enclosure is for this certificate, not for $V_\infty$. The physical bracket is instead
\[
0.017568198744<V_\infty(P_{1/20})\leq(19/20)\log20\approx2.84595.
\]
An explicit certificate, the best certificate in a selected observation family, and the unknown physical optimum are different quantities. The gap to the ring competitor is not an error estimate for the lower certificate.

\subsection{Bounded observational uncertainty}\label{sec:robust}
\begin{corollary}[A conservative uncertainty-box certificate]\label{cor:robust}
Suppose the true stationary statistics obey $p\geq p_0>0$, $a\geq a_0\geq0$, $b\leq b_1$, and $\eta=a_0^2-b_1>0$. Then exponential clocks of rate $z$ give $\sig(Q)\geq16zp_0\eta^2$. For fixed windows of length $t$ on each side, $\sig(Q)\geq16p_0\eta^2/t$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every valid law in the box has $a^2-b\geq\eta$. Use $I(a,b)\geq8(a^2-b)^2$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate} or Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed}; boundary cells follow by continuity.
\end{proof}
For the $q=1/20,z=6$ witness, allow absolute errors at most $\varepsilon=1/10000$ in each of $p,a,b$. The choices $p_0=2/3-\varepsilon$, $a_0=18180/19861-\varepsilon$, $b_1=329212800/394459321+\varepsilon$ give $\eta>0$ and a strictly positive rational floor, verified by the extension checker. This is a deterministic sensitivity statement. A confidence claim additionally needs simultaneous coverage for this box, justified for the observation scheme and temporal dependence; no such statistical procedure or sample-size guarantee is asserted here. A box with $\eta\leq0$ supplies no positive certificate by this test, not evidence of equilibrium.

\section{A broader zero-cost dichotomy}\label{sec:dichotomy}
The positive floor is not restricted to this example. A complete dwell distribution is called \emph{hyperexponential} when its density is a finite positive mixture of exponential densities, $\sum_j\alpha_j\lambda_j e^{-\lambda_jt}$, with $\alpha_j>0$, $\sum_j\alpha_j=1$, and $\lambda_j>0$.

\begin{theorem}[No unattained zero limit for finite-realizable alternating renewal]\label{thm:dichotomy}
Let $P$ be a stationary ergodic two-label alternating-renewal law that has a finite irreducible CTMC realization with deterministic observation. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=0pt]
\item $V_\infty(P)=0$;
\item both complete dwell distributions are hyperexponential;
\item $P$ has a finite reversible exact realization.
\end{enumerate}
Thus growing architecture cannot approach zero entropy production without a finite zero-cost optimizer anywhere in this class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The implication (iii)$\Rightarrow$(i) is immediate. To prove (i)$\Rightarrow$(ii), choose exact realizations $Q_k$ with $\sig(Q_k)\to0$, and replace each by $R_k=(Q_k+Q_k^\dagger)/2$. For either observed block $B$, the stationary mass is the same fixed $p$. By \eqref{eq:pathkl} and Pinsker's inequality,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closure}
 |H_{R_k}(t)-H_P(t)|\leq\sqrt{t\sig(Q_k)/8}.
\end{equation}
The killed generator $(R_k)_{BB}$ is self-adjoint in $L^2(\pi|_B)$ and strictly negative definite. Here $\pi|_B$ is the unnormalised restricted measure, of mass $p$, not the conditional probability measure. Its spectral theorem gives
\[
 H_{R_k}(t)=\langle\one,e^{t(R_k)_{BB}}\one\rangle_{\pi|_B}
 =\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-\lambda t}\,\mu_k(d\lambda),
 \qquad \mu_k([0,\infty))=p,
\]
with a finite positive discrete measure $\mu_k$.

For completeness, the limit retains a positive spectral measure. Compactify $\lambda\in[0,\infty)$ by the coordinate $x=e^{-\lambda}\in(0,1]$. A subsequence of the corresponding measures on $[0,1]$ converges weakly. For each $t>0$, $x^t$ is continuous, so \eqref{eq:closure} identifies $H_P(t)$ with its limiting integral. Continuity $H_P(t)\to p$ as $t\downarrow0$ excludes mass at $x=0$. Also $H_P(t)\to0$ as $t\to\infty$ excludes mass at $x=1$. Hence
\[
 H_P(t)=\int_{(0,\infty)}e^{-\lambda t}\,\mu(d\lambda)
\]
for a positive finite measure of mass $p$.

Now use finite realizability of the fixed target, not of the approximating reversible sequence. For any one finite target realization, $H_P(t)=\pi_Be^{tQ_{BB}}\one$, so $H_P$ satisfies a nonzero constant-coefficient differential equation $P_0(\partial_t)H_P=0$, by Cayley--Hamilton. Differentiating the integral at $t>0$ is legitimate because polynomial factors times $e^{-\lambda t}$ are bounded. Thus
\[
 \int P_0(-\lambda)e^{-\lambda t}\,\mu(d\lambda)=0\qquad(t>0).
\]
The finite signed measure $e^{-\lambda}P_0(-\lambda)\mu(d\lambda)$ has zero Laplace transform. Uniqueness of that transform implies it is zero. Therefore $\mu$ is supported on the finitely many positive roots of $P_0(-\lambda)$, and $H_P$ is a finite positive mixture of exponentials.

For a stationary complete dwell density $f_B$ and entrance intensity $r_B$, the visit-length identity is $H_P''(t)=r_B f_B(t)$. If $H_P(t)=\sum_j c_j e^{-\lambda_jt}$, the density weights are $\alpha_j=c_j\lambda_j/r_B>0$: indeed $f_B(t)=\sum_j\alpha_j\lambda_j e^{-\lambda_jt}$ and $\sum_j\alpha_j=-H_P'(0)/r_B=1$. Apply the same argument to $A$ to prove (ii).

Finally suppose (ii) holds, with densities $\sum_i\alpha_i\lambda_i e^{-\lambda_it}$ in $A$ and $\sum_j\beta_j\mu_j e^{-\mu_jt}$ in $B$. Introduce states $A_i,B_j$ and rates
\[
 Q_{A_iB_j}=\lambda_i\beta_j,\qquad
 Q_{B_jA_i}=\mu_j\alpha_i,
\]
with no within-block jumps. Set
\[
 r=\left(\sum_i\alpha_i/\lambda_i+\sum_j\beta_j/\mu_j\right)^{-1},
 \quad \pi_{A_i}=r\alpha_i/\lambda_i,
 \quad \pi_{B_j}=r\beta_j/\mu_j.
\]
Every cross-edge has flux $r\alpha_i\beta_j$ in both directions. The chain is irreducible and reversible. Each exit chooses the next block's phase independently from its specified mixture weights. This gives the exact independent alternating-renewal law, proving (iii).
\end{proof}

The renewal hypothesis is used for the sufficiency construction. For a general observed process, hyperexponential marginal dwell laws alone do not determine the complete path law and are not asserted to guarantee a reversible realization.

\subsection{Beyond the elementary variance obstruction}\label{sec:beyondvariance}
Let the $A$ dwell be exponential of rate one and let the $B$ dwell be the equal mixture of the $q=1/4$ ring dwell and an exponential of rate $1/100$. Its density is
\[
f_B(t)=\frac{81}{160}e^{-3t/4}-\frac{49}{160}e^{-7t/4}+\frac1{200}e^{-t/100}.
\]
An exact finite irreducible realization, with the first state labelled $A$ and the other three labelled $B$, is
\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}
-1&2/5&1/10&1/2\\
1/4&-5/4&1&0\\
1&1/4&-5/4&0\\
1/100&0&0&-1/100
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The unique $A$ state resets independent visits. All used edges have reverse support, so its dissipation is finite. Direct integration gives $\E T_B=254/5$ and $\E T_B^2=70016/7$, hence
\[
\operatorname{CV}_B^2=\frac{324697}{112903}>1.
\]
The elementary necessary equilibrium condition $\operatorname{CV}^2\geq1$ therefore does not exclude this law. Nevertheless its Laplace transform has the negative residue $-49/160$ at $-7/4$. Uniqueness of the Laplace transform excludes any positive exponential-mixture representation. Theorem~\ref{thm:dichotomy} implies $0<V_\infty(P)<\infty$. This example demonstrates the classification's reach; it does not assign a numerical optimum or assert that the two-test certificate detects every nonhyperexponential law.

\section{Interpretation, verification, and claim limits}
A stationary alternating-renewal observation can be time-reversal symmetric even when every compatible Markov mechanism dissipates. The obstruction here is not an observed directional current. It is a restriction imposed by conditional independence and reversibility of the microscopic mechanism. Mixing equilibrium hidden states produces nonnegative past--future correlation for the two persistence tests, whereas $P_q$ has the strictly negative covariance \eqref{eq:deltaq}. The rate comparison \eqref{eq:randomkl} converts that separation into a uniform thermodynamic cost.

The inherited and additional implications should be distinguished. Tu~\cite{tu} connects nonequilibrium switching to dwell-time restrictions. Skinner and Dunkel~\cite{skinner}, Supplement Section II, equations S6--S10 (A6--A10 in the HTML rendering), derive the equilibrium positive exponential-mixture restriction and credit an earlier antecedent. Their waiting-time programme includes hidden-model optimization and a variance--dissipation curve. The $q=1/20$ target already has $\operatorname{CV}_B^2=481/842<1$, so recognizing it as nonequilibrium is not the new conclusion. Ehrich~\cite{ehrich} studies fitting hidden mechanisms for discrete-time partial observations and discusses enlarging the hidden network. Nitzan, Ghosal, and Bisker~\cite{nitzan} optimize transition and waiting-time statistics with a hypothesized hidden topology. Neither numerical convergence in explored dimensions nor fixed-dimension exclusion is used here as a proof of a positive unrestricted infimum.
